# Introduction

Anomalous body marks have been used as evidence for supernatural encounters since at least the 1500s with the witches' and devil's marks.  Alien abduction researchers in the 1980/90s presented as supporting evidence, most notably as a criteria for the UFO Abduction Syndrome defined in the 1992 booklet Unusual Personal Experiences.  The booklet was published by Robert Bigelow, who later ran the governments UAP study program disclosed in the famous 2017 New York Times article *Glowing Auras and 'Black Money'* and also supported research into alien implants.  This document introduces D-SETI's Body Mark Extractor tool as a first step of producing a database of these marks for research purposes.

There is no central database of body mark cases, although some individual researchers or groups of experiencers publish their datasets.  Alien abduction body marks were primarily documented by Budd Hopkins in his notes, with only a handful of images publicly available now through his books or recorded lectures.  Karla Turner was an experiencer and researcher who collected experience reports and photographs, publicly available through her books and recorded lectures.  The tradition of experiencer-researcher continues with Mike Clelland, Nancy du Tertre, and Jay Anderson who all reported triangular body marks and write on topics related to alien/NHI encounters.  Most researchers document the mark and its association with events, but can not explain its appearance.  Some researchers and groups focus on specific types of marks like the Red Grid Mark Phenomenon (RGMP) or the Target Symbol Mark Phenomenon (TSMP).

Like Vallee, I wondered why the work of interstellar doctors would leave scars, then hypothesized the marks may be communication in the universal language of geometry. I published an integral survey of body marks as my [[Field Guide to Anomalous Geometry]], which directly arose from my own geometric body mark experience and my academic writing about the hypothesis.  I surveyed multiple mark types, the abduction researchers public records (I suspect there's a trove in the Archives of the Impossible), and social media for sources of reports.  

In the Field Guide, I extracted the marks through a manual process, printed them out, then draw geometric observations on the image.  The process was guided by dreams associated with my geometric body marks.  They taught me how to make meaning from the marks using geometry as a meditation tool.   I used this method to observe that some marks, like Paul Vidal's DIAL mark, might be a word or letter of an encoded message sent across all DIAL marks like Stan Romenak's or else in similar geometry like in the King's or Kitei's Phoenix Lights footage.  

D-SETI's Body Mark Extractor is the first step to building a database of geometric body marks that may constitute a SETI-style signal sent through dreams. # Geometric Inquiry is Both Objective and Subjective

This tool extracts body marks using techniques the author in the production of his book [[Field Guide to Anomalous Geometry]], which used a standard procedure to document and study dozens of anomalous body marks.  This tool accomplishes a labor intensive step of testing the hypothesis that anomalous geometric body marks may be a SETI-style signal.

This tool enables two paths of inquiry:
1. **Objective research** into these marks by producing images that are easily read by computer vision programs
2. **Subjective meaning making** by producing images that can be printed and used in creative process

The Field Guide defined as way to make meaning from the marks as omens and psycho-spiritual events.  After producing the abstract black and white images, the next step in analysis is geometric observation, in which the author attempts to produce a ruler-and-compass diagram of the mark.  

Once such a drawing is produced, the figure may be described in terms of a single circle, which is understood to be a mandala or a psycho-spiritual container for a contemplative event.  With the Extractor, you are able to crop the output image to the mark and rotate the image, which will produce a square image based on the mark's widest dimension.  This ensure that images produced in this way can be compared with one another just by dropping them in a slide show or measuring light/dark ratios using computer vision.

# FAQ

This tool is designed to facilitate reporting and study of anomalous geometric body marks that people often report with dreams or dream-like experiences by extracting the mark from a photo.

## What does this tool do?
This tool extracts an image of a body mark from a photograph, producing a high contrast version of the mark completely extracted from its personal context. 

## Why should I use this tool?
This tool produces a clear black and white image that can be printed and studied.  You may then print out the mark and draw on it or analyze its geometry.

The final image is abstracted from the image of your skin, so you can share an image of the mark without revealing your private parts

## What are anomalous body marks?
Anomalous body marks are marks that you discover, but do not remember the marking or the memories involve anomalous events.  The most famous types of marks include alien abduction marks, demon marks, and so on.  This tool was made with the Red Grid Mark Phenomenon in mind.

Typically, the experiencer seeks an explanation for the mark because of its strangeness or accompanying intuition or memory about an encounter.

Such marks have been used for centuries as evidence for supernatural encounters.

## How should I photograph my marks?

You should photograph your marks in clear light from straight on.  If it is on your back or a hard to reach place, a tripod and self-timer could help you or a friend.  Try to keep the skin neutral and not stretched. 

Take multiple photographs over multiple days from several angles.  Include a coin next to, but not over, the mark for a scale reference in some but not all photos.


## Why doesn't this tool work for me and my mark?

There are several reasons why this tool wouldn't work.  First, there might be a bug in the software or something went down somewhere.  It is completely free and hosted using free cloud services, so there's no promise of uptime or service.

This tool was developed using an iterative process on images that were available, which means it was only made better when the author discovered a limitation or error in the extractions.  

Various marking types or skin tones may confuse the system, in which case you can email the author, daniel@dseti.org, with a sample photo and he will likely upgrade the system handle your case.
